Before the Southport defeat there was a three-nil win at home to Buxton. Across those five games that's ten goals for and six against, and every result and every scorer I've read straight off either the club's own site or a named local report, so that's as solid as this show gets.

Now the one from TheFA.com, because there's a genuine Law change live this season that touches a game like Saturday's directly. Delayed throw-ins and goal-kicks now carry a five-second visual countdown, and if the team taking it hasn't restarted play by the time it runs out, the restart's awarded to the opposition. That applies at every level including National League North — flag it to anyone on your side who likes to slow a game down at a throw-in, because it can now cost the ball outright rather than just a word from the referee. Two smaller changes alongside it this season: no caution any more for a stopped-attack foul if advantage is played and a goal still results, and a hard ten-second limit for a substituted player to actually leave the pitch. None of the VAR or goal-line technology changes making headlines at the top of the game apply at your level — those are competition-optional, and nobody's running them at Step Two, so anything you hear about that side of the Laws this season isn't relevant to Saturday.

The practical thing to take into the weekend, then: Spalding's goals across their last three have come in bursts rather than spread evenly, Cybulski's the name in form on one game's evidence, and the throw-in countdown is new enough this season to be worth a word with your set-piece takers before Saturday, whichever way the toss goes.
