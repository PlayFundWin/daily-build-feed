## The core fact Steve asked for: is any of this legally mandatory?

No. As of this date, nothing described below is a legal requirement for PlayFundWin.
Two separate voluntary schemes are in play, plus one binding-but-general code:

1. **Fundraising Regulator registration** (the "free draw operators" thread this show
   has tracked since Ep32) — voluntary. Registering, and meeting the proposed
   charitable-contribution threshold, only gates whether an operator may display the
   **Fundraising Badge**. Declining to register does not stop PlayFundWin operating.
2. **DCMS's Voluntary Code of Good Practice for Prize Draw Operators** — also
   voluntary, but DCMS has said explicitly that if voluntary take-up doesn't fix the
   problems it's meant to address, government will bring forward legislation. This is
   the sharper reason to stay ahead of it: it's a live hint about where a future
   mandatory regime would start from, not just a badge scheme.
3. **The Code of Fundraising Practice itself** (the PDF Steve supplied, 2025 edition) —
   explicitly states it "is not legally binding." It applies in full to organisations
   registered with the Fundraising Regulator and displaying the Fundraising Badge; the
   Regulator says it will still consider complaints about any UK charitable fundraising
   activity "whether registered with us or not," so the general standards of honest,
   fair, legal, transparent conduct effectively apply regardless of registration status
   — registration adds the badge and formal commitment, not the baseline duty to be
   honest and legal, which already exists under ordinary consumer/charity law.

## The registration-threshold thread (Ep32 onward)

- Consultation: "Consultation on free draw operators registering with the Fundraising
  Regulator," closed 3 April 2026.
- Proposed charitable-contribution threshold, phased: **10% of ticket
  sales/turnover for 2026/27, 15% for 2027/28, 20% from 2028/29** — eventually matching
  what society lotteries must already meet. Operators who can't demonstrate they meet
  the threshold "will not be eligible for registration" (i.e. can't get/keep the
  badge — this is not a fine or a ban on operating).
- Registration for free draw operators was paused while the consultation ran. The
  Regulator's own page said registration is due to reopen "in September" (2026). As of
  2026-09-16 it is not confirmed whether that has actually happened, or whether a final
  published position on the threshold exists yet — this is the open question worth a
  human checking directly (Steve was asked to do this in Ep37 and again in Ep39).
- Separately, a **DCMS voluntary code of good practice for prize draw operators**
  (distinct scheme, see below) took effect 2026-05-20 and requires signatories to also
  meet Fundraising Regulator standards where they apply — the two threads overlap.

### Stated benefits of registering (Fundraising Regulator's own reasoning)

- Right to display the **Fundraising Badge** in marketing and on-site.
- A documented, public commitment to the Code of Fundraising Practice.
- Third-party validation aimed at public/donor confidence — the Regulator's own
  framing for why this whole area needs tidying up is that inconsistent practice among
  prize-draw operators "can cause confusion for the general public about how much
  money goes to charities." Registering is PlayFundWin choosing to be legible and
  trusted in a category the regulator itself has flagged as currently confusing.

## The DCMS Voluntary Code of Good Practice for Prize Draw Operators

- Created by DCMS (not the Fundraising Regulator), issued in final form around
  20 November 2025; signatories had to comply by **20 May 2026**.
- Requires: age verification (18+), spend/credit limits (a £250/month figure has been
  cited), account-suspension tooling and harmful-behaviour monitoring, transparent draw
  rules and odds disclosure "where possible," guaranteed prize award, visible
  charitable-contribution disclosure, internal compliance systems, and oversight of any
  third-party partners.
- Voluntary — breaches aren't legally enforceable — but DCMS's own language warns that
  if this doesn't resolve the problems identified, "the Government will bring forward
  legislation." Treat this as the real reason to track the thread: it's a preview of
  what a mandatory regime would likely require, published by the department that would
  write it.

## Prize competitions and free draws — general standards (Code of Fundraising
## Practice, Section 11, effective 1 November 2025)

Applies to a charitable institution or third-party fundraiser running a prize
competition or free draw, regardless of registration status:

- Must have written authorisation from the charitable institution to fundraise this way.
- Must publish the organiser's name and clear, simple access to full terms and
  conditions; must follow the CAP Code (Code of Non-broadcast Advertising and Direct &
  Promotional Marketing) for promotional marketing.
- The draw: all valid entries must be included, the draw must be random and the result
  recorded — witnessed if done in person, or via a verifiable random-number process if
  electronic. Entrants must be told in advance how the draw will be carried out.
- After the draw: keep ticket/payment records for audit; take all reasonable steps to
  contact every winner within seven days; take all reasonable steps to actually award
  prizes; publish winners' details unless they objected when entering.
