Part two. The build.

Today's build idea is a voice notes app, and I want to walk you through this one properly, because the evidence behind it is unusually solid and the story is a good one.

In twenty twenty three, a self-taught developer based in Bangkok called Nico Jeannen built something called TalkNotes. The idea is almost embarrassingly simple once you hear it: you record a voice memo on your phone, and the app turns it into a structured written output, a blog post, a set of bullet points, a polished email draft, whatever format you ask it for.

Here are his actual published numbers, and I want to be clear these are attributed figures from Jeannen himself, not my estimates or anyone's marketing copy. Fifteen hundred dollars in total revenue within twenty days of launch, back in September twenty twenty three. Now, an honest flag before we go further, because I promised you honesty over hype on this show. Those numbers are from twenty twenty three and twenty twenty four, not this year. I'm treating this as a proven pattern rather than a live, fresh case study, and you should too when you're deciding how much weight to put on it. The underlying mechanics haven't changed at all, though, and if anything the tools available to build something like it have got considerably easier and cheaper since he first built it by hand.

Here's why this is buildable in days rather than months, which is really the whole point of this segment. There's no machine learning expertise required anywhere in this stack, none at all. You need a simple record-or-upload interface for the user, a call out to an existing speech-to-text service to turn audio into raw text, a prompt to an AI language model that restructures that raw transcript into whatever format the user asked for, a payment system so people can actually pay you, and a basic login so people have accounts. Jeannen built the original with fairly basic web tools before later moving it onto a proper framework as it grew. Every single piece of that stack is something Claude can scaffold for you directly and quickly: the front end people see, the payment integration, the login and authentication, the API calls out to the transcription and language model services. The actual intelligence in the finished product is entirely off-the-shelf, bought in from other companies' AI models. You are not training anything yourself. You are wiring together services that already exist and already work.

Practically, that breaks into roughly four sittings if you're working with Claude Code directly rather than hiring anyone. Sitting one is the recording interface and the transcription pipeline, getting audio in and text out reliably, which is mostly plumbing. Sitting two is the prompt that reshapes the transcript, and this is genuinely the only part worth obsessing over, because a mediocre prompt gives you a mediocre product no matter how good the underlying model is. Sitting three is accounts and payment, wiring in Stripe and basic login, which by now is close to a solved problem for any AI coding assistant. Sitting four is the boring but essential bit, an actual working demo you can put in front of five real people in your target niche and watch them use it, before you spend another hour polishing something nobody asked for.
